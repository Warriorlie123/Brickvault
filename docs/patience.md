# Patience: the only edge a player has

[probability.md](probability.md) shows that crates are a losing bet.
[markets.md](markets.md) shows that prices move. Put them together and you get
the one real strategy in BrickVault: **open less, wait more, sell well.**

## The impatient player

- Opens crate after crate, chasing the next Legendary.
- Sells everything the moment it lands, at whatever the market pays.
- Takes a loan when the balance runs low.

In simulation, a player who simply opens Series 1 and sells instantly goes broke
**every single time**, usually within 77 openings. The impatient player is
exactly what the house edge is designed for.

## The patient player

- **Waits for the price.** A figure bought into a bearish phase can be worth
  1.5–2× in a later bullish phase. Prices are capped at 2.2× base value, so there
  is real upside, but only for someone willing to hold.
- **Sells in pieces.** Spreading sales out avoids pushing your own price down.
- **Waits for the buyer.** Collector offers and BrickBid auctions regularly pay
  above market. They show up over time, not on demand.
- **Completes sets.** Finishing a theme raises its values by 10 % for good. That
  reward goes to collectors, not flippers.
- **Avoids debt** unless the repayment plan doesn't depend on luck.

None of this changes the odds of a single crate. Patience can't beat the house
edge on an opening, but it can beat the *market* on a sale, and the sale is the
only part of the game the player controls.

## Delayed gratification

Behavioral economics has a name for preferring a small reward now over a larger one
later: **present bias**. Loot boxes, slot machines and day-trading apps all exploit
it. They are fast, colorful, and they reward the action of *trying* rather than
the outcome.

BrickVault uses the same tricks on purpose: spinning reels, rarity sounds,
near-miss strips. Then it puts the counter-strategy in plain sight. The fastest
way to lose is to keep pressing the button. The way to win is to let time do
the work.

## Takeaways

1. You can't control the drop, but you can control when and how you sell.
2. Time can be an asset or a cost. Waiting helps your sales, and loan interest punishes waiting.
3. If a game rewards *speed*, ask who it's really built for.
